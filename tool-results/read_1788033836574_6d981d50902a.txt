     9→     9→## Q1. Ammonia synthesis equilibrium — Gillespie-Beattie & alternatives
    10→    10→
    11→    11→### Gillespie-Beattie (1930) correlation — RECOMMENDED for the engine [S][V]
    12→    12→For reaction ½N2 + (3/2)H2 ⇌ NH3, defined as:
    13→    13→
    14→    14→    Kp = p_NH3 / (p_N2^0.5 * p_H2^1.5)      [atm^-1],  partial pressures in atm, T in K
    15→    15→
    16→    16→    log10(Kp) = 2074.8/T − 2.4943*log10(T) − beta*T + 1.856e-7*T^2 + I
    17→    17→
    18→    18→beta and I are pressure-dependent (apparent Kp in partial-pressure terms; the pressure
    19→    19→dependence absorbs fugacity effects):
    20→    20→
    21→    21→| P (atm) | beta        | I      |
    22→    22→|---------|-------------|--------|
    23→    23→| 10      | 0           | 1.993  |
    24→    24→| 30      | 3.4e-5      | 2.021  |
    25→    25→| 50      | 1.256e-4    | 2.090  |
    26→    26→| 100     | 1.256e-4    | 2.113  |
    27→    27→| 300     | 1.256e-4    | 2.206  |
    28→    28→| 600     | (in original table; out of scope for a 150-250 bar app) |
    29→    29→
    30→    30→Sources: Gillespie & Beattie, Phys. Rev. 36, 743 (1930) — data spanning 325–952 °C;
    31→    31→coefficient table reproduced in El-Gharbawy et al. (2021), Egypt. J. Chem. 64(5),
    32→    32→https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1110062120304670
    33→    33→and https://www.researchgate.net/publication/348338338 (table snippet confirms
    34→    34→10/30/50/100/300 atm rows exactly).
    35→    35→
    36→    36→**Numerical verification performed in this research** (solving equilibrium NH3 mol% for
    37→    37→stoichiometric H2/N2 = 3 feed with the above formula, linear interpolation of beta/I in P):
    38→    38→
    39→    39→| T      | 100 atm | 150 atm | 200 atm | 300 atm |
    40→    40→|--------|---------|---------|---------|---------|
    41→    41→| 350 °C | 37.3 %  | 45.2 %  | 50.9 %  | 59.1 %  |
    42→    42→| 400 °C | 25.2 %  | 32.4 %  | 38.2 %  | 47.0 %  |
    43→    43→| 450 °C | 16.3 %  | 22.3 %  | 27.4 %  | 35.8 %  |
    44→    44→| 500 °C | 10.5 %  | 15.0 %  | 19.1 %  | 26.4 %  |
    45→    45→
    46→    46→These match the classic Larson–Dodge / Vancini textbook tables (e.g., 450 °C/100 atm ≈ 16 %,
    47→    47→400 °C/200 atm ≈ 38 %, 500 °C/300 atm ≈ 26 %) → **implementation is correct as written above,
    48→    48→including signs.**
    49→    49→
    50→    50→Validity ranges: T ≈ 325–950 °C, P ≈ 10–300 atm (source data). Interpolate beta, I linearly
    51→    51→in P between table rows; do NOT extrapolate above 300 atm.
    52→    52→WARNING: units are **atm**, and Kp uses the ½N2 + (3/2)H2 convention (Kp in atm^-1). If the
    53→    53→reaction is written N2 + 3H2 ⇌ 2NH3, then Kp² of the half-convention is used.
    54→    54→
    55→    55→### Alternatives

    58→    58→- El-Gharbawy (2021) — simple fitted correlation for Kp, 350–600 °C, 10–300 atm; claims
    59→    59→  close match to G-B; useful as a cross-check. [S]
    60→    60→- Vancini, C.A., "Synthesis of Ammonia", Macmillan (1971) — classic book with the G-B
    61→    61→  tables (citation confirmed: https://ouci.dntb.gov.ua/en/works/455xpDv4). [S]
    62→    62→- Appl, M., "Ammonia: Principles and Industrial Practice", Wiley-VCH (1999); also Ullmann's
    63→    63→  "Ammonia, 2. Production Processes" chapter. [S]
    64→    64→- Utwente 2025 "Thermodynamics and kinetics for ammonia synthesis" — enhanced thermodynamic
    65→    65→  model + unified kinetic model (open PDF:
    66→    66→  https://research.utwente.nl/files/495064044/1-s2.0-S1385894725054294-main.pdf). [S]
    67→    67→- **"Temperley/Gillespie" appears in the student's plan — no "Temperley" correlation for
    68→    68→  NH3 synthesis Kp exists in the literature (searched; only aqueous-ammonia unrelated hits).
    69→    69→  Likely a hallucinated/garbled name. The lineage is Larson-Dodge → Gillespie-Beattie →
    70→    70→  Vancini/Appl.** [S]
    71→    71→
    72→    72→### Approach-to-equilibrium values used in industry
    73→    73→- Per-bed **fractional approach to equilibrium** in modern converters: design convention
    74→    74→  **0.90–0.95** (e.g., Cheema & Krewer 2018 keep each bed outlet at 773 K or "90% of the
    75→    75→  equilibrium temperature": https://pubs.rsc.org/ra/article/8/61/34926/619808). [S]
    76→    76→- The plan's "60–70% fractional approach" is LOW for modern plants — it will underpredict
    77→    77→  converter outlet NH3 by ~3–5 percentage points. Old Kellogg quench converters achieve
    78→    78→  ~85–90%; modern radial (Topsoe S-300, Casale, KBR) ~90–95%. Recommend default 0.90 per
    79→    79→  bed (or temperature approach 15–30 °C). [D, partially S via Cheema]
    80→    80→- Alternative convention — temperature approach: ATE = T_out − T_eq(outlet composition).
    81→    81→  For the primary reformer with good catalyst: ATE(CH4) 0–5 °C good, 5–10 °C acceptable,
    82→    82→  >10 °C marginal (Zečević & Bolf, Processes 8:408, 2020,
    83→    83→  https://www.mdpi.com/2227-9717/8/4/408). [S]

    87→    87→## Q2. Temkin-Pyzhev kinetics & converter types
    88→    88→
    89→    89→### Rate equation (Dyson-Simon 1968 activity form of Temkin-Pyzhev) [S]
    90→    90→Used in essentially all modern HB reactor models (Cheema 2018, Solmaz 2024, Flórez-Orrego 2017):
    91→    91→
    92→    92→    r_NH3 [kmol NH3 / (m3 cat . h)] =
    93→    93→        2*k * [ K_a^2 * a_N2 * (a_H2^3 / a_NH3^2)^alpha  −  (a_NH3^2 / a_H2^3)^(1−alpha) ]
    94→    94→
    95→    95→where a_i = activity (or partial pressure) of species i, K_a = equilibrium constant (activity
    96→    96→basis), alpha ≈ 0.5–0.75, and k follows Arrhenius: k = k0 * exp(−Ea/(R*T)).
    97→    97→
    98→    98→Constants reported:
    99→    99→| Parameter set | k0 | Ea | alpha | Validity |
   100→   100→|---|---|---|---|---|
   101→   101→| Dyson & Simon (1968), industrial Fe | 8.849e14 kmol/(m3·h) | 170,560 kJ/kmol (40,765 kcal/kmol) | 0.5 | 149–309 atm, 603–768 K (Nielsen data) |
   102→   102→| Solmaz/Nadiri refit (2024) | 6.5e13 | 159.4 kJ/mol | 0.654 | 90 bar lab, 598–773 K |
   103→   103→| Montecatini catalyst (used by Flórez-Orrego 2017, backward rate) | k0b = 2.57e14 | Eab = 163,500 kJ/kmol | 0.55 | 150–300 atm, ±10–20% deviation |
   104→   104→
   105→   105→Sources: https://pure.mpg.de/rest/items/item_3608464_2/component/file_3608958/content
   106→   106→(Solmaz 2024, ChemCatChem — full PDF read: alpha=0.654, k0=6.5e13, Ea=159.4 kJ/mol);
   107→   107→https://infoscience.epfl.ch/server/api/core/bitstreams/dc58e187-417b-4e2d-961e-112df2c5be18/content
   108→   108→(Flórez-Orrego ECOS 2016, Table 1); 8.849e14 & 40,765 kcal/kmol confirmed in multiple
   109→   109→sources (Tyrański 2023 CFD paper; SSRN preprints); Nielsen 1964 (J. Catal.): Temkin-Pyzhev
   110→   110→applicable 370–495 °C and 150–310 atm.
   111→   111→
   112→   112→### When kinetics vs equilibrium-only
   113→   113→- **First build: equilibrium + fractional approach (0.90–0.95) per bed.** Reproduces outlet
   114→   114→  compositions within ~1–2 % NH3 of plant data, is deterministic, and avoids catalyst-volume
   115→   115→  data. Add adiabatic temperature rise per bed (ΔH_rxn = −46 kJ/mol NH3, gas Cp mixing).
   116→   116→- **Kinetics worth adding (v2)** for: bed temperature profiles, catalyst loading, turndown /
   117→   117→  part-load (Power-to-Ammonia) behavior, H2/N2≠3 off-stoichiometry. Dyson-Simon is the
   118→   118→  industry-standard "good enough" kinetic set; deviations 10–20% in rate are common, so
   119→   119→  calibrate alpha/k against one plant anchor point. [D + S]
   120→   120→
   121→   121→### Converter technology landscape [S]
   122→   122→- Kellogg multibed **axial quench** converter — classic 1000+ MTPD single train (first
   123→   123→  1000 t/d plant: Mississippi Chemical, Yazoo City, mid-1960s; UNIDO 1982 "Reactor designs
   124→   124→  and catalysts from ammonia plants" documents Kellogg quench vs radial designs:
   125→   125→  https://www.unido.org/publications/ot/9646198/pdf).
   126→   126→- Modern: **radial-flow** converters — Topsoe S-200 (2-bed), **S-300 (3-bed radial,
   127→   127→  intercooled)**; Casale axial-radial; KBR (Kellogg Brown & Root) advanced ammonia process
   128→   128→  with Ru catalyst (KAAP). Topsoe product pages:
   129→   129→  https://www.topsoe.com/solutions/technologies/equipment-and-spare-parts/ammonia-converter-baskets-s-300
   130→   130→- Uhde/thyssenkrupp: **3 radial magnetite beds in 2 converters**, loop 190–200 bar,
   131→   131→  HP steam generation downstream of each bed (IFA 2004, Frisse:
   132→   132→  https://www.fertilizer.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/2004_tech_beijing_frisse.pdf — full PDF read).
   133→   133→- Modeling: 3 adiabatic beds with interbed cooling (quench or indirect) is the right
   134→   134→  granularity; bed inlet 380–430 °C, bed outlet ≤ 500–530 °C (hot spot limit, magnetite).
   135→   135→  Cheema design: all bed inlets 673 K (400 °C), outlets 773 K (500 °C) at 200 bar. [S]

   139→   139→## Q3. Reforming equilibrium & SMR conditions
   140→   140→
   141→   141→### Equilibrium constants (ideal gas, Dalton's law — adequate at 25–40 bar) [S][V]
   142→   142→Reactions:
   143→   143→    SR1 (steam reforming):  CH4 + H2O ⇌ CO + 3H2        ΔH°298 = +206 kJ/mol
   144→   144→    SR2 (water-gas shift):  CO + H2O ⇌ CO2 + H2         ΔH°298 = −41 kJ/mol
   145→   145→
   146→   146→    K_SR1 = (p_CO * p_H2^3) / (p_CH4 * p_H2O)  = 1.198e13 * exp(−26830/T)   [bar^2]
   147→   147→    K_WGS = (p_CO2 * p_H2) / (p_CO * p_H2O)    = 1.767e-2 * exp(+4400/T)    [dimensionless]
   148→   148→(p in bar, T in K.) Source: Zečević & Bolf, Processes 8(4):408 (2020)
   149→   149→https://www.mdpi.com/2227-9717/8/4/408 (also free at
   150→   150→https://psecommunity.org/wp-content/plugins/wpor/includes/file/2006/LAPSE-2020.0560-1v1.pdf).
   151→   151→
   152→   152→**Verification in this research (Python):**
   153→   153→- K_WGS at 400 °C → 12.2; at 220 °C → 133 — matches standard WGS tables. [V]
   154→   154→- K_SR1 = 1.198e13·exp(−26830/T) at 800 °C/30 bar/S/C=3 gives equilibrium dry CH4 slip
   155→   155→  8.1 %, H2 71.6 %, CO 9.5 %, CO2 10.7 % — squarely in the industrial primary-reformer
   156→   156→  range (CH4 slip 5–9 % dry). [V] (NOTE: the MDPI PDF typesets the pre-exponential as
   157→   157→  "1.198×10^17"; that is a typo/extraction artifact — 10^13 reproduces industrial slip,
   158→   158→  10^17 gives nonsense. Trust 1.198e13.)
   159→   159→- Alternative model: RGibbs (Gibbs energy minimization) with RK-Soave EOS was used by the
   160→   160→  Rice CENG403 design project for both reformers — also valid; ideal-gas Kp is simpler and
   161→   161→  adequate. [S]
   162→   162→
   163→   163→### Typical SMR operating conditions [S]
   164→   164→- Front-end (reforming/purification) pressure: **25–35 bar** (EFMA booklet; Rice used 35.3 bar).
   165→   165→- S/C molar ratio: **≈ 3.0** (EFMA: "around 3.0 for BAT processes"; Rice: 3:1 optimal —
   166→   166→  2.5 raises CH4 slip & carbon risk, 4.0 raises duty; hydrogen plants use higher, 5+).
   167→   167→- Feed desulfurization: feed-gas up to 5 mg S/Nm³; hydrogenation + ZnO to <0.1 ppm S.
   168→   168→- Mixed feed preheated to 500–600 °C in convection section before tubes.
   169→   169→- **Primary reformer outlet: 780–830 °C** (EFMA); general SMR literature 800–900 °C at
   170→   170→  20–30 bar (globalsyngas.org: 815–925 °C). Tube metallurgy limits front-end to ~40 bar.
   171→   171→- Primary reformer duty example (1000 MTPD, Rice): 50 MMkcal/h (≈58 MW), 230 tubes, 4 in
   172→   172→  ID, 35 ft long.
   173→   173→- Only **30–40 % of hydrocarbon is converted in the primary** (EFMA) — the rest converts in
   174→   174→  the secondary.
   175→   175→- **Secondary reformer**: process air (21 % O2) compressed to reforming pressure, preheated
   176→   176→  ~600 °C; adiabatic — partial combustion of H2/CH4 raises gas to **~950–1000 °C** (Rice
   177→   177→  optimum 996.2 °C); exit residual CH4 **0.2–0.3 % dry** (EFMA; ~0.7 % indicates air
   178→   178→  distribution problems — ammoniaknowhow.com). Exit gas contains **12–15 % CO (dry)**
   179→   179→  (EFMA; ammonia-plant-fundamentals slide deck: ~13 % CO, 7.3 % CO2).
   180→   180→- Secondary outlet cooled to 350–400 °C in waste-heat boiler/superheater generating
   181→   181→  HP steam (~100 bar, 450–510 °C).
   182→   182→- Secondary modeling recipe: (1) adiabatic combustion zone — O2 consumed by H2 (fastest)
   183→   183→  with overall heat release fixing temperature; (2) equilibrium (SR1+SR2) over catalyst with
   184→   184→  small CH4 approach. Air flow is the design DOF set by final H2/N2 = 3. [D+S]
   185→   185→- Sources: EFMA "Booklet No. 1 Production of Ammonia" (open PDF — full text read):
   186→   186→  http://productstewardship.eu/fileadmin/user_upload/user_upload_prodstew/documents/Booklet_nr_1_Production_of_Ammonia.pdf ;
   187→   187→  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steam_reforming ; https://globalsyngas.org/syngas-technology/syngas-production/steam-methane-reforming ;
   188→   188→  Rice CENG403: http://www.owlnet.rice.edu/~ceng403/nh3ref97.html
   189→   189→
   190→   190→### Approach-to-equilibrium used industrially (front end) [S]
   191→   191→- Primary reformer: methane ATE 0–5 °C (good catalyst), 5–10 °C acceptable, >10 °C marginal
   192→   192→  (Zečević 2020). Design shortcut commonly 10–25 °C. [S/D]
   193→   193→- WGS: equilibrium-limited; HTS exit ~3 % CO dry, LTS exit 0.2–0.4 % CO dry (EFMA).
   194→   194→- Secondary reformer: near-equilibrium on CH4 (residual 0.2–0.3 % dry); temperature approach
   195→   195→  typically 20–40 °C on CH4 equilibrium. [D]

   199→   199→## Q4. Water-gas shift
   200→   200→
   201→   201→| Item | HTS | LTS |
   202→   202→|------|-----|-----|
   203→   203→| Catalyst | Fe2O3/Cr2O3 (Fe-Cr) | CuO/ZnO (Cu-Zn, +Al2O3) |
   204→   204→| Inlet T | ~350–400 °C (JM: inlet ≈350 °C) | 200–220 °C |
   205→   205→| Exit CO | ~3 % dry (equilibrium-limited at operating T) | **0.2–0.4 % dry** (well-run plants 0.1–0.3 %) |
   206→   206→| Model | equilibrium K_WGS (above) + approach 10–25 °C | equilibrium K_WGS + approach ~15–30 °C |
   207→   207→
   208→   208→- Inlet CO to HTS: 12–15 % dry (from secondary reformer).
   209→   209→- Steam/dry-gas ratio at LTS inlet ≈ 0.45–0.50 mol/mol (cheresources practitioner data).
   210→   210→- Interbed cooling between HTS and LTS (boiler feedwater preheat).
   211→   211→- Catalysts are poisoned: HTS by chlorides/silica; LTS by S, Cl (Cu).

   219→   219→## Q5. CO2 removal & methanation
   220→   220→
   221→   221→### CO2 removal
   222→   222→- Candidate processes: MEA (legacy, high regeneration energy — NOT BAT), **aMDEA (BASF
   223→   223→  activated MDEA — current default for new plants)**, **Benfield** hot potassium carbonate
   224→   224→  (promoted; variants HiPure, LoHeat), Selexol (physical), PSA (H2 plants). [S — EFMA]
   225→   225→- Residual CO2 after removal: **100–1000 ppmv typical; down to ~50 ppmv achievable** [S — EFMA].
   226→   226→  Modern aMDEA designs run < 50 ppmv (researchgate: "CO2 slip < 50 ppmv" for new systems).
   227→   227→- Heat consumption (chemical absorption): **30–60 MJ per kmol CO2** [S — EFMA]. Physical
   228→   228→  solvents ≈ 0 heat but mechanical energy.
   229→   229→- Removal efficiency ≈ 99.5 %+ of the CO2 (CO2 is ~18–22 % dry of shifted gas) [D].
   230→   230→- Absorption column ~30–60 bar (front-end pressure), regeneration at 1.5–2 bar.
   231→   231→- Modeling for first build: black-box splitter with fixed residual ppm + reboiler duty
   232→   232→  30–60 MJ/kmol CO2; VLE not needed. [D]
   233→   233→
   234→   234→### Methanation (final purification)
   235→   235→- Ni catalyst, **~300 °C** inlet (EFMA; literature 300–450 °C); adiabatic.
   236→   236→- Reactions: CO + 3H2 → CH4 + H2O; CO2 + 4H2 → CH4 + 2H2O.
   237→   237→- Temperature rise: **74 °C per 1 vol% CO converted, 60 °C per 1 vol% CO2** [S — Johnson
   238→   238→  Matthey methanation brochure:
   239→   239→  https://matthey.com/documents/161599/440146/JM%20Methanation%20product%20brochure%20%281%29.pdf].
   240→   240→- Exit CO+CO2: **< 1 ppm** (must be; oxygenates are reversible poisons to the Fe synthesis
   241→   241→  catalyst — total oxygenates should stay below ~10 ppm, normally single-digit ppm). [S/D]
   242→   242→- Methanator inlet total carbon oxides: ~0.2–0.4 % CO + 0.01–0.1 % CO2 → CH4 added to loop
   243→   243→  inerts + H2 consumed (4:1 on CO2, 3:1 on CO) + water knocked out after cooler.
   244→   244→- Sources: EFMA booklet; JM brochure; MDPI Catalysts 13(2):448 (methanation review).

   284→   284→## Q7. Syngas compression (polytropic)
   285→   285→
   286→   286→### Configuration [S]
   287→   287→- Make-up syngas: from front-end 25–35 bar to loop 150–200 bar (up to 250 bar);
   288→   288→  **2 casings (LP+HP), typically 3–4 compression stages with intercooling to ~40 °C**,
   289→   289→  steam-turbine driven (HP/MP extraction or condensing). Uhde: two-casing compressor pairs
   290→   290→  with 190–200 bar loop (IFA/Frisse). Example: Puertollano syngas 25→250 bar, 2 trains
   291→   291→  (idom.com). Older designs: single 4-case train to 153 bar (Amhamed 2022 review).
   292→   292→- Stage pressure ratio typically 2–3.5; keep discharge T ≤ ~150–200 °C (H2-rich gas).
   293→   293→- Recycle circulator: small ΔP (5–25 bar boost), centrifugal, η ≈ 75 %.
   294→   294→- Refrigeration compressor (NH3), 2-stage with intercooling, η ≈ 75 %.
   295→   295→
   296→   296→### Closed-form polytropic equations (ideal gas) [S]
   297→   297→    (n − 1)/n = (k − 1)/(k * η_p)                    (compression; k = Cp/Cv)
   298→   298→    H_p = Z_avg * R * T1 * (n/(n − 1)) * [ (P2/P1)^((n−1)/n) − 1 ]     [J/kmol]
   299→   299→         = (Z_avg * R_u * T1 / M) * ...                               [J/kg with R = Ru/M]
   300→   300→    W_gas = mdot * H_p                                [W]
   301→   301→    T2 = T1 * (P2/P1)^((n−1)/n)
   302→   302→
   303→   303→with η_p = polytropic efficiency (0.72–0.78 for large centrifugal syngas machines; 75 %
   304→   304→used in the Flórez-Orrego model), Z_avg ≈ mean compressibility (H2-rich syngas Z ≈ 1.02–1.08;
   305→   305→H2 has Z>1 — do not assume Z<1). k for 3:1 H2/N2 ≈ 1.40–1.41 at 300 K (H2 1.41, N2 1.40),
   306→   306→lower at temperature.
   307→   307→Sources: http://docs.codecalculation.com/thermodynamics/chap06.html (head formula);
   308→   308→https://www.jmcampbell.com/tip-of-the-month/2011/11/compressor-calculations-rigorous-using-equation-of-state-vs-shortcut-method ;
   309→   309→https://www.scribd.com/document/294062820/Polytropic-Compression (W = m*Hp/ηp note);
   310→   310→75 % efficiency: Flórez-Orrego [S]. Efficiency range 72–78 % [D].
   311→   311→
   312→   312→Sanity anchor: 1000 MTPD plant, syngas make-up ≈ 4,900–8,300 kmol/h compressed 25→150 bar
   313→   313→consumes ~8–10 MW; total loop power ~13 MW optimal. [S — Flórez-Orrego]

   358→   358→## Q9. Recycle convergence (tear streams)
   359→   359→
   360→   360→- **Direct substitution**: x_{k+1} = g(x_k). Robust, linear convergence; for the NH3 loop
   361→   361→  (inert accumulation) typically 10–30 iterations to 1e-4 relative tolerance. Use for the
   362→   362→  first 2–5 iterations to get into the basin. [S/D]
   363→   363→- **Wegstein (1958)**: secant acceleration of fixed point. Per variable:
   364→   364→      s = (g(x_k) − g(x_{k−1})) / (x_k − x_{k−1))    (slope)
   365→   365→      q = s / (s − 1)
   366→   366→      x_{k+1} = q*x_k + (1 − q)*g(x_k)
   367→   367→  Typically converges in 5–15 iterations. **Failures/limits**: when s → 0, q → 0 →
   368→   368→  overshoot/oscillation; commercial simulators bound q (Aspen-type bounds q ∈ [−5, 0];
   369→   369→  DEM damping 0–5; see Wiley "Experimental methods... process flowsheeting" 2020,
   370→   370→  https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/cjce.23857 and classic Wegstein method PDFs).
   371→   371→  Apply damping (e.g., accelerate only after iteration 3; cap |q|). [S]
   372→   372→- **Broyden quasi-Newton**: rank-one Jacobian updates, superlinear; best for multi-variable
   373→   373→  tears (the loop tear has 6–8 variables: N2, H2, NH3, CH4, Ar, CO2(0), flows + recycle
   374→   374→  split). Needs more memory/code but handles strong coupling (converter ⇄ separator ⇄ purge).
   375→   375→  [S]
   376→   376→- **Fallback**: dynamic relaxation — Luyben (2004) "Use of dynamic simulation to converge
   377→   377→  complex process flowsheets" (https://www.cache.org/sites/default/files/fall2004_dynamicsimulation.pdf)
   378→   378→  — integrate the loop ODEs to steady state when Wegstein/Broyden oscillate. [S]
   379→   379→- **Recommended strategy for the app**: direct substitution with 0.5 damping for 3
   380→   380→  iterations → bounded Wegstein (q clipped to [−5, 0]) → if not converged in ~25 iters,
   381→   381→  switch to Broyden or halve the step. Tolerances: 1e-4–1e-5 relative on tear molar flows;
   382→   382→  expect total ~10–20 evaluations of the loop. [D + S]
   383→   383→- Physical tip that matters more than the solver: initialize the loop tear stream
   384→   384→  *close* to the answer (e.g., 13 % inerts, 2.5 % NH3, H2/N2 = 2.7, T = 380 °C, P = loop P);
   385→   385→  a cold start with pure 3:1 H2/N2 and zero NH3 wastes iterations and can drive the
   386→   386→  equilibrium solve to a trivial root. [D]

   398→   398→  = 2 % of global final energy, 1.3 % of energy-system CO2). [S]

   402→   402→  theoretical; more than half of the excess is compression losses (EFMA). [S]

   411→   411→- Steam drives: syngas compressor, process air compressor (24.5 MW at 3300 MTPD — Uhde),
   412→   412→  refrigeration compressor, BFW pumps via steam turbines; net steam export common (e.g., to
